# Clarifying questions — the sweep

Work through every area before concluding that the idea is complete. The questions are prompts, not
a script: skip every one the idea already answers, ask the rest in the operator's language and in
whatever order the conversation takes, and ask more where an answer is thin. An area that produces no
answer is named in the gate-1 report and recorded under `Assumptions and open questions`.

## The idea itself

- What is this game, in two or three sentences?
- Who is it for?
- What is the one thing it must do well?

## Genre and reference games

- What genre is it, in the terms the industry uses?
- Which existing games is it like? *Like X, but Y.*
- For each reference: what does this project take from it?
- For each reference: what does it deliberately not take?
- Is there a game it must not be mistaken for?

## Core loop and mechanics

- What does the player do, second to second?
- What do they get for doing it?
- What sends them round the loop again?
- Which mechanics are essential, and which would the client miss least?
- What can the player do that nothing else in the genre allows?

## Win, lose and failure

- What ends a run in the player's favour?
- What ends a run against them?
- Can the game be lost at all? If not, is that deliberate?
- What happens after a loss — restart, retry, continue?

## Content scope, in numbers

- How many levels, screens or stages?
- How many enemy types?
- How many items, weapons or abilities?
- How many characters the player can control?
- Which of these numbers is the client sure about, and which is a guess?

## Session shape

- How long is one session meant to last?
- Is progress saved? At what points?
- What carries between sessions — unlocks, scores, story position?
- Is there an end, or does it continue indefinitely?

## Input and platform

- Keyboard, mouse, touch, gamepad — which must be supported?
- Is one of them primary?
- Where must it run — browser, desktop, phone, console?
- Is there a device, screen size or orientation it must fit?

## Constraints and non-goals

- What has the client already decided, and required?
- Is there a deadline, a budget or an event this is for?
- Are there assets, brands or existing systems it must use?
- What is explicitly out of scope?
- What would the client consider scope creep?

## Success and risk

- How will the client judge the finished game?
- What would make them call it a failure even if it works?
- What worries them most about this project?
- What has been tried before, here or elsewhere, and did not work?

## Before you conclude

- Which fields is the client certain about, and which are guesses?
- Which answers came from the operator rather than the client?
- Is any answer a technical decision that belongs to the architect instead?
- Does anything the client said contradict anything else they said?
