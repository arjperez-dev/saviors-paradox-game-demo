This file holds the whole truth of the story, including what the demo must never say. §4 is the rule
every line of the demo is held against.

## 1. Logline

Akeris wakes from a vision: someone has used the Time Clock, his people's sacred relic, and the world
has days left. He goes down into the ancient dungeon whose last floor holds the clock, not knowing
that the one who used it is himself.

## 2. The world

| Thing | What it is |
|---|---|
| Akeris's people | an old people, unnamed in the demo, who kept the Time Clock in a sanctuary and prayed to it. They are gone from the game: only their buildings, their graves and their spirits remain |
| the Time Clock | the relic that holds time in place. Whoever turns it bends time; it corrupts whoever keeps it |
| the dungeon | 20 floors under the sanctuary, older than Akeris's people. Its last floor is the Time Chamber, guarded by the Time Guardian |
| the reset | whenever an Akeris dies in the dungeon, time starts over: Akeris wakes again from the vision, and the dungeon rebuilds itself into a new shape. This is the story's reason for permadeath and for procedural zones |
| the spirits | ancient spirits of the dungeon who live through every reset. They remember every Akeris who came down; Akeris remembers none of them |

## 3. The truth (full game)

1. There are three Akeris, three timelines of the same man, told apart by the colour of the eye streak:
   - **the Savior** (red streak, torn cape with holes): the player, at the start of the loop;
   - **Future Akeris** (red streak, fully torn cape, curved battered sword, very dirty): an Akeris from
     further along who has seen where the descent ends, and tries to stop the Savior reaching the last
     floor;
   - **Corrupted Akeris** (purple streak, holds the clock): an Akeris who reached the Time Chamber,
     took the clock and was corrupted by it. **He is the one who used the clock.** He waits on the
     last floor.
2. Akeris is both the cause of the world's end and its salvation, and neither ever happens: the world
   is always ending and never ends, because each death resets time.
3. The sacred sword Akeris always carries, without knowing what it is, has three gem-shaped hollows.
   Each successful incursion finds one gem. Only with the three gems can the most powerful Corrupted
   Akeris be beaten.
4. The spirits tell this piece by piece across incursions. Only in the third incursion does Akeris
   understand that he is fighting himself.

## 4. What the demo tells

The demo is zones 1 to 5 of the first incursion. Its reveal level is **suspicion**: nothing is said
outright, but a player who listens starts to guess that the one below is Akeris himself.

| The demo may say | The demo never says |
|---|---|
| the spirits have seen Akeris before, many times, and he has always come back | that Corrupted Akeris or Future Akeris is Akeris |
| the dungeon changes every time he comes | the word *paradox*, *timeline* or *reset* in a player-visible line |
| others came down with a light in their eye, and one of them came back with another colour | the colour *purple* next to the word *Akeris* |
| someone waits at the bottom, holding the clock | who used the clock |
| the sword has three hollows meant for something | that the hollows are for gems, where the gems are, or that three incursions are needed |
| someone watches Akeris from the battlements and does not want him to go on | why Future Akeris wants to stop him |
| one descent is not enough | how the full game ends |

The intro shows a silhouette like Akeris with only its purple streak visible; the outro shows Future
Akeris. Neither names them. Both are the demo's two strongest hints and they must stay images, not
words.

## 5. Voices

- **Akeris never speaks.** He has no dialogue lines, no barks, no answers to choose. The player's
  choices in a dialogue are neutral menu labels (`dialogue/rules.md` §4), not his words.
- **The spirits** speak an ancient language (a processed voice, `07-audio/voice.md`); what they say is
  shown as subtitles in the chosen language.
- **The cinematics** have no voice: images, sound and a few text cards.
- Every player-visible line is a translator key with an English and a Spanish text of the same tone
  (`00-game/tone.md` §1).
